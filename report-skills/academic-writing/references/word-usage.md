Precision passes.

## Weak nouns (interrogate every use)

ability · activity · analysis · approach · case · character · concept ·
context · degree · development · dynamics · effect · element · environment ·
event · factor · forcing · influence · interaction · issue · level ·
manner · nature · perspective · process · relationship · role · sense ·
situation · system · thing · use

Fixes: "the less stable **nature** of the lower troposphere" → "the lower
troposphere is less stable"; "a favorable **factor** for cyclogenesis" →
"favoring cyclogenesis"; "a variety of **factors** play a **role** in why
X" → "X occurred for the following three reasons…".

## Commonly misused terms (field-general entries)

- **accuracy vs skill** — accuracy: correspondence with observations;
  skill: improvement over a baseline (climatology, persistence). An
  accurate forecast may be unskillful and vice versa.
- **causing** — causal chains are usually inferred, not known; prefer
  "associated with". Statistical association ≠ causation (nearly every
  criminal has eaten a pickle); demand a plausible mechanism first.
- **chaos / random** — precise technical meanings; for messy patterns say
  "disorganized".
- **correlate/correlation** — only when a correlation coefficient was
  computed; else relate / correspond. r ≠ r².
- **data** — plural; "datum" or "data point" for one; reserve "data" for
  observations, not model output.
- **date vs day**; write dates unambiguously ("10 December 1994", never
  12/10/94).
- **dynamics** — vague hand-waving; name the actual process.
- **frequency** — must be per unit time; otherwise "number of events".
- **methodology** — means the study of methods; usually you want
  "methods".
- **objective/subjective** — automated methods embed subjective choices
  too; say automated / manual.
- **observed/seen** — only with direct measurements; otherwise "occurred",
  "the simulation did not produce…".
- **percent vs percentage** — percent is the unit (%); percentage =
  fraction.
- **propagate** — technical-sounding synonym for *move*; movement =
  advection + propagation; say which you mean, usually "move".
- **resolution** — prefer "grid spacing/interval"; a model resolves
  features no smaller than ~2Δx.
- **significant** — statistical significance only (report test, statistic,
  dof, p); otherwise it misleads.
- **state** — means "declare definitively" (state a hypothesis), not "say".
- **claim** — connotes disbelief in the person; use with care.
- **theory** — reserved for time-tested frameworks; a paper's proposal is a
  hypothesis.
- **why vs how** — science answers how; philosophy answers why. "Why the
  formation varies…" → "How the formation varies… has not been determined."
- **adjective–noun agreement** — high/low temperature (not warm/cold);
  large/small velocity (not fast/slow); qualitative adjectives for objects
  only.
- **hemisphere-neutral** — poleward/equatorward; cyclonic/anticyclonic
  vorticity.
- **t test** — Student's t test (Gosset's penname).
- **trigger** — with multi-ingredient phenomena, whichever ingredient
  arrives last is "the trigger"; the concept misleads — name the missing
  ingredient instead.

## Commas

1. After transitional/introductory elements (if removable, use a comma).
   But never separate a gerund/infinitive-phrase subject from its verb:
   "Choosing a structure for the wavelet is crucial…" (no comma).
2. Between independent clauses joined by a conjunction (short clauses may
   skip it).
3. Around nonrestrictive (which-type, removable) modifiers; restrictive
   (that-type) take none: "A calibration estimate, which is sufficient…"
   vs "A calibration estimate that brings the radar within 1–2 dB is
   sufficient…". Also: "Green Bay, Wisconsin, receives…".
4. Items in a series (most journals use the serial/Oxford comma).
5. Between coordinate adjectives replaceable by "and": "a stronger, more
   zonal flow".

## Hyphens

- Compound modifiers before a noun: cloud-to-ground lightning,
  cause-and-effect relationship; value+unit as modifier: "500-hPa wind" but
  "the wind at 500 hPa".
- Suspended hyphens: "lower- and upper-level anomalies"; "700-, 500-, and
  300-hPa temperatures".
- -ly adverbs take none: "slowly moving storm", "widely used scheme" (but
  "well-known equation" before its noun; "the equation is well known"
  after).
- Prefixes usually solid (reexamine, nonlinear, postfrontal); hyphenate for
  proper nouns (trans-Atlantic), triple letters (shell-like), ex- =
  former, or disambiguation (re-count vs recount).
- Spelled-out numbers (twenty-nine); single letter + noun (X-ray).
- When in doubt: journal style guide, dictionary, or Chicago Manual.

## En dash (–)

Joins equals or ranges, no surrounding spaces: NCEP–NCAR reanalyses,
0000–1200 UTC, 30%–70%, 1970–1999, pp. 112–119, air–sea interaction; and in
compound adjectives with a multiword element: Nobel Prize–winning research.
Also the minus sign: −14°C. (LaTeX: `--`.)

## Em dash (—)

Sets off contrast, examples, or interruptions — a stronger break than a
comma. It packs a punch; use it rarely so it keeps its force. (LaTeX:
`---`.)
